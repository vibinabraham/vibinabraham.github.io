<div class="research-vision">
I develop many-body and quantum-computing methods that make the spectroscopy of molecules and materials <strong>predictive</strong>: from core-level X-ray spectra and heavy-element chemistry to strongly correlated systems. The aim is methods that are accurate, systematically improvable, and ready for both classical supercomputers and emerging quantum hardware.
</div>

    <h3>X-ray and core-level spectroscopy</h3>
    <div class="tags"><span class="tag pnnl">PNNL</span></div>
    <p><strong>Core ionization is local, but the electrons that screen the hole are not.</strong> I develop efficient classical (coupled-cluster) and quantum (QSP) methods that capture the many-body satellites in X-ray spectra, and use them to show how neighboring molecules and proton motion reshape screening.</p>
    <p class="key-papers">Key papers: <a href="https://pubs.aip.org/aip/jcp/article/164/10/104113/3383265/Elucidating-many-body-effects-in-molecular-core">J. Chem. Phys. 2026</a> · <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22268">arXiv:2608.22268</a></p>

    <h3>Quantum computing for chemistry</h3>
    <div class="tags"><span class="tag pnnl">PNNL</span><span class="tag vt">Virginia Tech</span></div>
    <p>Quantum computers promise to solve electronic structure problems that are out of reach classically, but only if algorithms are robust to noise and tested on problems that matter. I have developed equation-of-motion algorithms for excited states and response properties on near-term devices, and now work on chemically decisive benchmarks and uncertainty quantification to judge when quantum hardware delivers reliable answers.</p>
    <p class="key-papers">Key papers: <a href="https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2023/sc/d2sc05371c">Chem. Sci. 2023</a> · <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10813">arXiv:2601.10813</a> · <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14392">arXiv:2607.14392</a></p>
  </div>

<div class="research-card no-fig">
  <div>
    <h3>Hybrid quantum–classical many-body methods</h3>
    <div class="tags"><span class="tag pnnl">PNNL</span></div>
    <p>Practical many-body methods always truncate the correlation model, and that truncation can make them unreliable. I develop a combined quantum–classical, dual-space formulation that improves accuracy when the correlation model is incomplete, while keeping a variational bound on the energy.</p>
    <p class="key-papers">Key paper: <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04387">arXiv:2609.04387</a></p>
  </div>
</div>

    <h3>Relativistic Green's function methods for molecules and solids</h3>
    <div class="tags"><span class="tag umich">Michigan</span></div>
    <p>Heavy elements, including actinides, are central to catalysis, nuclear chemistry, and quantum materials, yet relativistic effects make them hard to model. I developed and benchmarked fully self-consistent, relativistic GW for ionization potentials and total energies of heavy-element molecules and periodic solids at finite temperature, implemented in the open-source <a href="https://green-phys.org/">Green</a> package.</p>
    <p class="key-papers">Key papers: <a href="https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/acs.jctc.4c00075">JCTC 2024</a> · <a href="https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2024/fd/d4fd00043a">Faraday Discuss. 2024</a> · <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010465524003035">Comput. Phys. Commun. 2024</a> · <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31571">J. Phys. Chem. Lett. 2026</a></p>

    <h3>Tensor product state methods for strong correlation</h3>
    <div class="tags"><span class="tag vt">Virginia Tech</span></div>
    <p>Strongly correlated systems, such as bond breaking and extended π-systems like nanographenes, defeat standard methods because the number of important configurations explodes. By grouping orbitals into clusters and working in a basis of many-particle cluster states, tensor product state methods (TPSCI, cluster many-body expansion) describe these systems compactly, for ground and excited states.</p>
    <p class="key-papers">Key papers: <a href="https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/acs.jctc.0c00141">JCTC 2020</a> · J. Chem. Phys. 2021 · JCTC 2022 · J. Phys. Chem. A 2023</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="research-card no-fig">
  <div>
    <h3>Singlet fission for solar energy</h3>
    <div class="tags"><span class="tag vt">Virginia Tech</span></div>
    <p>Singlet fission splits one absorbed photon into two triplet excitons, a route to solar cells beyond the usual efficiency limit. I derived a simple rule predicting when multiexciton states in covalently linked dimers are bound, and uncovered the competition between exchange and energy-transfer couplings in correlated triplet-pair states.</p>
    <p class="key-papers">Key papers: J. Phys. Chem. Lett. 2017 · J. Phys. Chem. Lett. 2021</p>
